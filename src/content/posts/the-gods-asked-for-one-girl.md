Somewhere tonight, a man is washing his brother's blood off his hands, and thanking God for the water.

The holy law. Three thousand years ago, the Greeks lived by it. Zeus gave it to them. Revelation.

From opening the door to strangers to searching for gods in a beggar's disguise. It is holy all the way, till it reaches the hypocrisy of men.

A thousand ships. No wind. The sea lay still as a grave, as the king Agamemnon ached for Troy, murdered his own child as a sacrifice. The goddess demanded it. Then the wind came, ships sailed, and history wrote it down, laid it all at Zeus's feet.

Ten years later, Troy burned. A priestess begged her goddess for mercy. A sword answered. Two heads fell: hers, and the statue's. Another sacrifice in the name of Zeus, keeper of the law of mercy. Holy hypocrisy. <span class="key">Holy hands, bloody knife.</span> Three thousand years on, the knife has only changed hands.

A god of peace. A god of mercy. A god who blesses. So why does every blessing need a body? Is it the god's will, or the man's hunger, borrowing the god's voice?

Three thousand years later, the altars have moved indoors. New names, same knife. Under every flag, from the Crusades to the world wars, from every genocide to this morning's headlines, men still kill men in the name of a merciful god, in every faith. Every god in history has been merciful. Ask the graves. God has never killed anyone. He just signs every death certificate. <span class="key">Nobody ever killed for a god. They killed for themselves, and sent him the bill.</span>

Now we know what runs in our blood. A plague. Blaming others for our mistakes. A disease we pass down like the colour of our eyes. It was never us. It was the other man. The other tribe. The other god. And when we ran out of others, we blamed heaven. So stop praying for blessings. You've been sending God your bills for three thousand years and long before, so whatever comes back won't be a blessing. It'll be a curse, with your name on it.

Somewhere tonight, a man is washing his brother's blood off his hands, and thanking God for the water.
