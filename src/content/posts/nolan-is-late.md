Somewhere right now Christopher Nolan is making breakfast, and he has started with the dishes.

The man cannot help it. Give him a story with a beginning, a middle and an end, and he will hand it back to you shuffled, reversed and folded into a shape mathematicians lose sleep over.

## A short history of a long obsession

In *Memento* he told a story backwards, so we could all feel what it is like to forget why we walked into a room. In *Inception* he made an hour last a week, so a nap became a career. In *Interstellar* he sent a father into a black hole and left us to explain relativity to our crying friends in the car park. In *Tenet* he stopped explaining altogether. Bullets went back into guns. Audiences went back into lobbies, asking each other what just happened.

Even *Oppenheimer*, a film about a real man and a real bomb, was cut like a clock that had been dropped down the stairs. History, it turns out, is also non-linear if you edit it hard enough.

> Most directors fight the clock. Nolan dates it, marries it, and then refuses to tell you what year the wedding was.

## The Möbius strip

Here is the thing, though. Take a strip of paper, twist it once, tape the ends. Walk along it and you end up where you started, except you are now on the other side. Same road. Different person.

That is what his films really do to you. You come out the same door you went in, and you are not quite the same person who went in. Annoying. Brilliant. Late, every time, and somehow exactly on time.

Chris, if you are reading this, please read it from the bottom up. You will enjoy it more.
